# Source Triage

> Audit every file in your pack for recency, scope, and motive before trusting any of it. (~5 minutes)

## research_mode
Source Auditing | Stress-testing provenance, recency, scope coverage, and author bias before letting data into the analysis

## context

Your data pack is below. Download each file, and open everything before trusting anything.

## files

- Client_Satisfaction_Tracker.xlsx
- Support_Ticket_Summary.docx
- Relationship_Manager_Email.docx
- Industry_Benchmark_NPS.docx

## source_lens
1. Current?|When was it written, and has the context, team, or stakeholder changed since?
2. Scope Match?|Does it measure the right service lines/population, or an apples-to-oranges comparison?
3. Evidence Backed?|Does the author have a stake in one conclusion without hard data?

## do_this

1. **Open every file.** For each one, write down in one line: What is this document? Who created it? When? What is its purpose?
2. **Run each file through the 3-step verification lens above.** Use your AI tool (Claude, Copilot, ChatGPT, etc.) to help assess each document: "How current is this source? Does it measure the right scope? Could the author have a reason to present things in a biased way?"
3. **As a group, sort each file** into one of three categories:
   - **Use** — reliable and relevant
   - **Use with caution** — has useful information but has limitations
   - **Discard** — outdated, biased, or not relevant enough to rely on
4. **Write a one-line reason** next to each file explaining your decision.

## prompting_guideline

Work one source at a time. Give the AI the document and your key question, and ask it to assess four things: how current the source is, what evidence backs its claims, whether its producer benefits from a particular conclusion, and whether it is actually relevant. Always ask for the single biggest reason to doubt the source; an assessment with no doubts is itself a red flag.

## tip

Not everything in your data pack is useful. In real client work, relationship managers send urgent emails that feel like data but are actually opinions. Part of this exercise is learning to say "we are not going to use this as primary evidence" and explain why.

## human_decision

Your AI tool can point out concerns, but the keep-or-discard decision is yours. Your group takes responsibility for what you use and what you leave out.

## checkpoint

Has your pod discarded or heavily discounted at least one source, in writing, with a reason you would say to its author's face?
