# Insight Framing

> A finding is what the data says. An insight is why it changes the stakeholder's decision. This phase turns one into the other. (~5 minutes)

## research_mode
Synthesis & Challenge | Converting raw findings about satisfaction scores into decision-ready insights for the account leader

## context

Your pod has profiled the data, tested hypotheses, and triangulated across sources. Now distill the noise into three sharp insights that the account leader can act on.

## do_this

1. Draft your candidate insights in the form: **we found X, it matters because Y, so you should consider Z.**
2. Run each through the AI as devil's advocate. Kill or sharpen anything that wobbles.
3. Converge on your best three. Fewer, sharper insights beat a long list every time.

## prompting_guideline

Cast the AI as the toughest executive in the room and give it your drafts. Useful challenges to request: is each one an insight or a restated fact, where is the weakest link in the evidence, and what question would it ask that you cannot yet answer? Invite bluntness; polite feedback is worthless in this phase.

## human_decision

Your three insights will shape how this account is managed — resource deployment, executive attention, commercial decisions. A wrong read wastes all three. Own what you put forward.

## tip

The strongest client health insights distinguish between things that need immediate action (e.g., a misaligned SLA) and things that need investigation (e.g., a low-response-rate survey). If all your insights feel equally urgent, you have not prioritised — push the AI to help you rank by impact and certainty.

## checkpoint

Read your three insights aloud. If any of them would make sense for any client in any industry, it is too generic — sharpen it or drop it.
