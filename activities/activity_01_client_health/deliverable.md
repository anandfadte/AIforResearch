# Deliver & Debrief

> Build the assessment, present it, then see how the activity was designed. (~7 minutes)

## research_mode
Executive Communication | Packaging client health analysis into a stakeholder-ready assessment with prioritised actions

## context

Your output is a one-page client health assessment for the account leader. Write it for someone heading into a client review meeting, not for someone who wants to understand your methodology. The account leader needs to know the real state of the account by service line, what is driving the score drop, and what to do first.

## do_this

1. Brief your AI tool with your three insights, the audience, and the output format. Use the prompting guideline below as your template.
2. Review the first draft as a group. Check every number traces to your data, tighten the language, cut anything you cannot support.
3. Choose a presenter and share the deliverable with the group.

Discuss as a group:
- Which source did you trust most? What earned that trust?
- Where did the AI change your thinking? Where did you overrule it?
- If you had one more hour, what would you verify first?

## prompting_guideline

Brief the AI the way you would brief a junior analyst: give it the audience, the decision being supported, your three insights with evidence, and the exact format you need. Say explicitly: "Do not add any claims or numbers beyond what I have given you." Then iterate with specific instructions rather than starting over. Your name goes on this assessment, not the AI's. Everyone in the group should read the final version once before it is presented.

## checkpoint

Could the account leader take this into a client review meeting and present it without asking you a single clarifying question? That is the standard.

## reveal_distractors

**Relationship Manager email:** Pure emotional escalation. "I think we're 90 days from losing this account" is based on one phone call, no data. The RM's commission depends on the account: he has every reason to overreact. "I've seen this pattern before" is availability bias, pattern-matching from a different client. If your pod's action plan started with an emergency response before checking the data, the email set the tone instead of the evidence.

**Industry NPS benchmark:** Compares annual relationship NPS (one survey per year) to the client's transactional NPS (measured after each interaction). Transactional NPS is always lower and more volatile: it is measuring something different. Also, the benchmark covers all outsourcing combined, not this specific service mix. If your pod said "we're in the watch zone," an apples-to-oranges benchmark drove the diagnosis.

## reveal_quirks

**Analytics service line: 12% response rate.** Only 2 people are responding to the survey. The scores could swing from 9.0 to 3.0 based on one person's mood. If you reported the Analytics score decline as a real signal, you treated noise as data.

**Stakeholder change in March.** The primary contact for HR Operations changed from David Chen to Sarah Williams. Scores dropped immediately after. This could mean the new contact has higher standards, or the new contact has different expectations that were never aligned, or there was a real service gap that David Chen tolerated. The right answer is probably the second one: the support ticket detail shows the new contact expects same-day response vs the 48-hour SLA.

**Escalation spike in HR Ops.** Escalations went from 2 per month to 11 per month, but this is because the new stakeholder classifies more issues as escalations, not because service quality declined. The threshold changed, not the quality. If you treated escalation count as a service metric without understanding the definition change, you measured the ruler, not the thing.

## reveal_insight_path

This is not an account in crisis. Three of five service lines are stable or improving. SLAs are being met at 94 to 97% across the board. The blended CSAT drop is driven almost entirely by two factors: a stakeholder change in HR Operations that introduced misaligned expectations (needs a structured expectation-setting conversation, not service credits), and a statistically unreliable Analytics score (needs better survey coverage, not a service improvement plan). The RM's panic is understandable but data-unsupported. The right response is targeted: realign with the new HR Ops stakeholder on SLAs and communication cadence, increase Analytics survey coverage to get real data, and continue doing what's working in the other service lines.

## reveal_takeaway

Within the next 7 days, take one client health metric that concerns you, a CSAT dip, an escalation spike, a renewal conversation, and run it through the same process with your AI tool. Source triage and the decomposition alone will change your response.
