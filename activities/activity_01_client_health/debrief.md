## reveal_distractors

**Relationship Manager email:** Pure emotional escalation. "I think we're 90 days from losing this account" is based on one phone call, no data. The RM's commission depends on the account — he has every reason to overreact. "I've seen this pattern before" is availability bias: pattern-matching from a different client. If your pod's action plan started with "emergency war room" before checking the data, the email set the tone instead of the evidence.

**Industry NPS benchmark:** Compares annual relationship NPS (one survey per year) to the client's transactional NPS (measured after each interaction). Transactional NPS is always lower and more volatile — it's measuring something different. Also, the benchmark covers all outsourcing combined, not this specific service mix. If your pod said "we're in the watch zone," an apples-to-oranges benchmark drove the diagnosis.

## reveal_quirks

**Analytics service line: 12% response rate.** Only 2 people are responding to the survey. The scores could swing from 9.0 to 3.0 based on one person's mood. If you reported Analytics' score decline as a real signal, you treated noise as data.

**Stakeholder change in March.** The primary contact for HR Operations changed from David Chen to Sarah Williams. Scores dropped immediately after. This could mean: (a) the new contact has higher standards, (b) the new contact has different expectations that weren't aligned, (c) there was a real service gap that David Chen tolerated. The right answer is probably (b) — the support ticket detail shows the new contact expects same-day response vs the 48-hour SLA.

**Escalation spike in HR Ops.** Escalations went from 2/month to 11/month — but this is because the new stakeholder classifies more issues as escalations, not because service quality declined. The threshold changed, not the quality. If you treated escalation count as a service metric without understanding the definition change, you measured the ruler, not the thing.

## reveal_insight_path

This is **not** an account in crisis. Three of five service lines are stable or improving. SLAs are being met at 94-97% across the board. The blended CSAT drop is driven almost entirely by two factors: (1) a stakeholder change in HR Operations that introduced misaligned expectations (needs a structured expectation-setting conversation, not service credits), and (2) a statistically unreliable Analytics score (needs better survey coverage, not a service improvement plan). The RM's panic is understandable but data-unsupported. The right response is targeted: realign with the new HR Ops stakeholder on SLAs and communication cadence, increase Analytics survey coverage to get real data, and continue doing what's working in F&A, IT Support, and Procurement.

## tip

The reflection matters as much as the deliverable. Client diagnostics are an ongoing discipline — the habits you build here determine whether you catch the next account risk early or react late.
