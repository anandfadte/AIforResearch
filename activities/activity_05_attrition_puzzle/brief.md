# The Brief

> Align on what the CHRO actually needs before anyone opens a spreadsheet. (~3 minutes)

## research_mode
Research Framing | Breaking a people-problem symptom into testable hypotheses about where attrition is concentrated and what is driving it

## scenario

You are working with the CHRO of a 650-person technology services company. Over the past 12 months, voluntary attrition has gone from 8% to 18%, more than doubling. The board meets in 48 hours.

Everyone in the leadership team has a theory. The CHRO thinks it is the return-to-office policy that came into effect in January. Sales leaders think it is compensation. Several managers have flagged career development. Nobody has looked at the data properly.

Your task is to find out where people are leaving from, why they are leaving, and what the company can realistically fix in the next 90 days. The CHRO does not want theories. She needs a retention brief she can take to the board.

## context

You have 4 files coming in Phase 2. Agree on the question first, before you touch any of them.

## do_this

1. Read the scenario out loud. As a group, write one sentence: what does the CHRO need to know before she walks into that board meeting? No AI tools until everyone agrees on the sentence.
2. Generate 4 to 6 specific, testable research questions. Push back if the first list feels generic.
3. Pick your top 2. These are the questions that, if answered with hard evidence, would directly shape the brief.

## prompting_guideline

Give your AI a research director role rather than a helpful assistant role. Paste the full scenario and ask it to generate testable hypotheses about where attrition might be concentrated. When the first list comes back, push on it: what would you actually need to find in the data to confirm or rule out each one?

## checkpoint

Before moving on, can everyone in the group state the CHRO's core question and your two priority research questions in one sentence without reading notes? The selection is yours to make. It shapes everything that follows.
