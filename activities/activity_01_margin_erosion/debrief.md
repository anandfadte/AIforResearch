## reveal_distractors

**Margin Improvement Playbook (2023):** This document was written before the company's Value Line product existed. Every strategy in it assumes the old product mix. Following its recommendations would be like using last year's map after the roads have changed. If your group relied on it, it likely sent you in the wrong direction.

**Regional Sales Head email:** The currency claim is mathematically true for Latin America (the Brazilian Real did drop 12%), but Latin America is only 15% of total revenue. The email takes a local truth and presents it as the global explanation. If your group accepted "it is all currency" without checking the revenue weight, the email set your direction instead of the evidence.

## reveal_quirks

**6 rows with negative cost of goods sold (COGS)** — these are returns that were miscoded as negative cost instead of revenue adjustments. If you did not spot them, your margin calculation is slightly off.

**Latin America revenue is in Brazilian Real (BRL)** while all other regions are in US Dollars — there is no flag in the data. If you compared LATAM revenue directly to other regions, you overstated it.

**Q3 column is labelled "Q2 (Restated)"** — a simple copy-paste error. If you did not notice, your quarter-over-quarter trend is wrong.

## reveal_insight_path

The margin drop is primarily a **product mix shift** story. The Value Line product (22% margin) grew 30% and took sales away from Premium Care (58% margin), which declined 18%. This mix shift alone explains approximately 1.6 of the 2.5 percentage point drop. Commodity cost increases explain about 0.5pp. Currency impact is real but small (approximately 0.4pp). The fixable part is product mix management — pricing strategy, channel separation, bundling. The structural part is that the Value Line exists and customers want it.
