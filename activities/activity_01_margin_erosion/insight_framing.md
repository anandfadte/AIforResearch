# Insight Framing

> Turn analytical findings into sharp, decision-ready insights the CFO can act on. (~5 minutes)

## research_mode
Evidence Synthesis | Compressing quantitative findings into decision-ready insight statements with clear evidence chains

## do_this

1. **Write your top findings** using this format: "We found [X]. It matters because [Y]. The CFO should consider [Z]." Write 3–5 of these.
2. **Stress-test with your AI tool.** Paste your insights and say: "Act as a tough, skeptical CFO. For each insight, tell me: Is this genuinely an insight, or just a restated fact? Where is the weakest evidence? What question would you ask that we cannot yet answer?"
3. **Narrow to your best 3.** Based on the AI's feedback and your own judgement, pick the three strongest insights. Fewer, sharper insights are always better than a long list.

## prompting_guideline

Give your AI tool a specific role when stress-testing. Say: "You are the toughest executive in the room. Be blunt. For each insight I share, tell me: (1) Is this an insight or just a restated fact? (2) Where is the weakest link in the evidence? (3) What would you challenge if this was presented to you?" Polite feedback is not useful here — you want direct pushback.

## tip

A good insight changes what the CFO decides to do. If your insight would be true for any company in any industry (e.g., "costs went up"), it is too generic. A strong insight is specific: it names the product, the region, the cause, and what can be done about it.

## human_decision

Your three insights will shape what the CFO tells the board. If the analysis is wrong, there is no "the AI told us." Your group owns what you put forward.

## checkpoint

Read your three insights aloud. If any of them would make sense for any company in any industry, it is too generic — sharpen it or replace it.
