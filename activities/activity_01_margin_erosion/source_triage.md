# Source Triage

> Audit every file in your pack for recency, scope, and motive before trusting any of it. (~5 minutes)

## research_mode
Source Auditing | Stress-testing provenance, recency, scope coverage, and author bias before letting data into the analysis

## context

Download all four files below and inspect each one before running any analysis. In real research, an inbox full of relevant-looking documents often mixes hard evidence with outdated playbooks or self-interested narratives.

## files

- Product_PnL_Extract.xlsx
- Commodity_Price_Index.docx
- Margin_Improvement_Playbook_2023.docx
- Email_Regional_Sales_Head.docx

## source_lens
1. Current?|When was it written, and has the context, team, or scope changed since?
2. Scope Match?|Does it measure the right cohort/population, or an apples-to-oranges slice?
3. Evidence Backed?|Does the author have a stake in one conclusion without hard data?

## do_this

1. **Open every file.** For each one, write down in one line: What is this document? Who created it? When? What is its purpose?
2. **Run each file through the 3-step verification lens above.** Use your AI tool (Claude, Copilot, ChatGPT, etc.) to help assess each document: "How current is this source? Does it measure the right scope? Could the author have a reason to present things in a biased way?"
3. **As a group, sort each file** into one of three categories:
   - **Use** — reliable and relevant
   - **Use with caution** — has useful information but has limitations
   - **Discard** — outdated, biased, or not relevant enough to rely on
4. **Write a one-line reason** next to each file explaining your decision.

## prompting_guideline

Work through one document at a time with your AI tool. Upload the file and say: "I am evaluating this source for a margin analysis. Please assess: (1) How current is this data? (2) Does it cover the right scope for our question? (3) Does the author benefit from a particular conclusion? (4) What is the biggest reason to doubt it?"

## tip

Not everything in your data pack is useful. In real work, inboxes are full of documents that look relevant but can actually mislead you. Part of this exercise is learning to say "we are not going to use this" and explain why. It is perfectly fine to set a document aside.

## human_decision

Your AI tool can point out concerns, but the keep-or-discard decision is yours. Your group takes responsibility for what you use and what you leave out.

## checkpoint

Has your group set aside or flagged at least one document, with a clear written reason? If everything looks equally trustworthy, look again more carefully.
