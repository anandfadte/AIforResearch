# Deep Research

> Profile the data first, run quantitative analytics, and triangulate against external evidence. (~10 minutes)

## research_mode
Analytical Deep-Dive | Profiling raw data for anomalies, running mix-shift and cost analytics, and triangulating against external benchmarks

## do_this

1. **Upload the Excel file to your AI tool.** Ask it: "Before analysing this data, please profile it first. Tell me: how many rows and columns, are there any blank cells, duplicate rows, inconsistent labels, or values that look wrong?" Review what it finds and agree on any cleanup needed.
2. **Test your two key questions.** Ask your AI tool to analyse the data against each of your top 2 research questions from Phase 1. Important: ask it to show its working (the numbers and calculations), not just the conclusion.
3. **Look for outside evidence.** Ask your AI tool to search for recent industry trends (commodity prices, consumer goods margin trends) that might support or contradict what the data shows. Where do the data and external sources agree? Where do they disagree?

## prompting_guideline

Always profile before you analyse. Say to your AI tool: "Before drawing any conclusions, list every data quality issue you find — blanks, duplicates, odd values, inconsistent formats. Wait for my instructions before proceeding." This prevents it from building conclusions on messy data.

## prompting_guideline_2

When a finding starts to seem true, test it from the opposite direction. Ask your AI tool: "Now try to find evidence that contradicts this conclusion. Is there any credible, recent data that would suggest a different explanation?" This keeps you from locking onto the first answer too quickly.

## tip

If your AI tool gives you a number, always ask "how did you calculate that?" Senior leaders get challenged on numbers in board meetings — make sure you can explain every figure in your analysis. If you cannot explain it, do not use it.

## human_decision

Every claim your group carries forward needs at least two independent pieces of evidence. If you only have one source for a finding, label it clearly as a "hypothesis" rather than a conclusion.

## checkpoint

Did the data profile in Step 1 reveal any issues? Did those issues change your analysis? If you skipped the profiling step, go back — it exists to prevent errors.
