# Deep Research

> Profile the data, test your hypotheses, and find the signal the exit surveys are hiding. (~10 minutes)

## research_mode
Analytical Deep-Dive | Joining workforce and compensation data to find where attrition is actually concentrated, and testing whether the narrative in the qualitative sources holds up

## do_this

1. Upload Workforce_Snapshot.xlsx to your AI tool. Before analysing, ask it to profile the data: how many rows, any blanks or inconsistencies, and are there any fields that look unreliable? Fix what needs fixing before proceeding.
2. Test your two research questions against the data. Ask the AI to show its working — not just the conclusion, but the numbers behind it. Where is attrition highest? Is it concentrated by department, level, tenure, manager, or location?
3. Now bring in Compensation_Analysis.xlsx. Ask your AI to compare the salary levels of employees who left against those who stayed, broken down by department and years of tenure. What does the gap look like for employees who have been with the company three to five years?
4. Cross-reference against Exit_Survey_Data.xlsx. Do the reasons people gave for leaving line up with what the numbers show? Where do the two sources agree, and where do they tell completely different stories?

## prompting_guideline

Always profile before you analyse. Tell your AI tool: "Before drawing any conclusions, tell me every data quality issue you find — blank fields, duplicate records, inconsistent labels, values that look wrong. Wait for my review before proceeding." A clean dataset is not guaranteed.

## prompting_guideline_2

When a pattern starts to look clear, test it from the opposite direction. Ask your AI: "Now try to find data that contradicts this conclusion. Is there any reading of this data that points somewhere else?" An explanation that only gets stronger when challenged is the kind worth taking to a board.

## human_decision

If the exit survey data and the workforce data point in different directions, your group needs to decide which one to trust more and explain why. That judgment call is yours.

## checkpoint

Do you have at least two independent pieces of evidence for your main finding? If you only have one source pointing in a direction, label it a hypothesis until you can verify it.
