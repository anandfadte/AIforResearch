# The Brief

> Align on the CTO's question and decompose it into researchable cost hypotheses. (~3 minutes)

## research_mode
Research Framing | Decomposing a cloud cost overrun into testable hypotheses about waste, pricing, and usage patterns

## scenario

Your client is a Series C SaaS company spending $2.8M per month on cloud infrastructure and vendor tools. Over the last year, cloud costs rose 42% while product usage grew only 18%. The gap between cost growth and usage growth is accelerating: last quarter alone, costs grew 14% while usage grew just 3%.

The CTO needs answers for the board: where is the waste? Is it orphaned resources, oversized instances, missed commitment discounts, shadow IT subscriptions, or something else, and how much can be saved in 90 days?

Her mandate to your pod: "Don't tell me to optimise. Tell me exactly what is costing us money for no reason and what to turn off first."

## context

You will receive a 4-file data pack in Phase 2. Before touching any files, align as a group on the exact question the CTO needs answered.

## do_this

1. Read the scenario out loud. As a group, write one sentence: what decision does the CTO actually need to make? No AI tools until everyone agrees.
2. Generate 4 to 6 categories of cloud cost waste. Push back if the first list feels like generic FinOps advice.
3. Pick your top 2 hypotheses. These are the waste categories that, if confirmed with data, would explain the largest share of the cost-usage gap.

## prompting_guideline

Give your AI a cost analyst role rather than a helpful assistant role. Paste the full scenario and ask it to surface testable hypotheses about where cost growth exceeds usage growth. When the first list comes back, push on it: what would you need to find in the billing data to confirm or rule out each one?

## checkpoint

If the CTO asked "what is your best guess before you look at the data?" could you give her a specific, testable answer? Not a category of waste — a mechanism. If not, your hypotheses are still too broad.
